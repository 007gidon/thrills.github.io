Friday the 13th

Friday the 13th is a 1980 American independent slasher film produced and directed by Sean S. Cunningham, written by Victor Miller, and starring Betsy Palmer, Adrienne King, Harry Crosby, Laurie Bartram, Mark Nelson, Jeannine Taylor, Robbi Morgan, and Kevin Bacon. Its plot follows a group of teenage camp counselors who are murdered one by one by an unknown killer while they are attempting to re-open an abandoned summer camp with a tragic past. Released on May 9, 1980, Friday the 13th was a major box office success, grossing $59.8 million worldwide. Critical response was divided, with some praising the film's cinematography and score, while numerous others derided it for its depiction of graphic violence. Aside from being the first independent film of its kind to secure distribution in the U.S. by a major studio, its box office success led to a long series of sequels, a crossover with the A Nightmare on Elm Street film series, and a 2009 series reboot.

  • Slasher Horror:

    "Friday the 13th" (1980) epitomizes the slasher subgenre with its iconic portrayal of terror and suspense. Directed by Sean S. Cunningham, the film centers on a group of camp counselors who are stalked and brutally murdered by an unseen assailant at Camp Crystal Lake. The film's masked killer, later revealed to be Mrs. Voorhees seeking revenge for her son's death, strikes fear into the hearts of viewers with each gruesome kill. With its tense atmosphere, inventive death scenes, and memorable final girl trope, "Friday the 13th" established itself as a quintessential slasher film, spawning a long-running franchise and influencing countless horror movies that followed.
