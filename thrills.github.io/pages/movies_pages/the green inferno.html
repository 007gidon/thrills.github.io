The Green Inferno

"The Green Inferno" is a 2013 horror film directed by Eli Roth. The movie follows a group of student activists who travel to the Amazon rainforest to protest against deforestation. However, their plane crashes deep in the jungle, and they find themselves captured by a remote tribe known for their cannibalistic rituals. As the group tries to escape from the tribe's clutches, they face increasingly gruesome challenges and must confront the horrors of the jungle. The film explores themes of survival, human nature, and the clash between civilization and primal instincts. "The Green Inferno" pays homage to classic exploitation films of the 1970s, particularly those set in remote and dangerous locations. It is known for its graphic violence, shocking imagery, and unflinching portrayal of the brutality of the tribe. The movie received mixed reviews from critics, with some praising its intense atmosphere and social commentary, while others criticized its excessive gore and controversial depiction of indigenous people. Overall, "The Green Inferno" is a provocative and unsettling horror film that pushes the boundaries of the genre, offering a visceral and unforgettable viewing experience for those willing to brave its disturbing content.

"The Green Inferno" embodies a unique blend of three distinct horror subgenres:

  • Cannibal Horror:

    The film is a prime example of cannibal horror, a subgenre that features tribes or groups of people who consume human flesh. In "The Green Inferno," the protagonists face the horrifying reality of being hunted by a cannibalistic tribe in the depths of the Amazon jungle.

  • Survival Horror:

    As the protagonists fight for their lives against the cannibal tribe and navigate the dangers of the jungle, "The Green Inferno" fits within the survival horror subgenre. The characters must use their wits and resourcefulness to outsmart their captors and escape the deadly situation.

  • Exploitation Horror:

    "The Green Inferno" draws inspiration from exploitation films of the 1970s, particularly those featuring graphic violence and taboo subjects. The film's portrayal of cannibalism and its unflinching depiction of gore and violence align it with the exploitation horror subgenre.
