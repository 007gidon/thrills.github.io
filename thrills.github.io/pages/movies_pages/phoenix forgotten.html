Phoenix Forgotten

"Phoenix Forgotten" is a 2017 American found footage science fiction horror film that marks the directorial debut of Justin Barber, who co-wrote the screenplay with T.S. Nowlin. Produced by Nowlin, Ridley Scott, Wes Ball, Courtney Solomon, and Mark Canton, the film was initially marketed under the title "Phoenix." Set against the backdrop of the widely reported 1997 UFO phenomenon known as the "Phoenix Lights," the story follows the mysterious disappearance of three teenagers who embark on a quest to uncover the truth behind the event. As they venture into the Arizona desert to investigate, they document their journey using video cameras. "Phoenix Forgotten" explores themes of curiosity, obsession, and the unknown, as the characters' quest for truth leads them into increasingly dangerous territory. The found footage format adds to the film's sense of realism, immersing viewers in the characters' experiences and heightening the tension as the mystery unfolds. Released on April 21, 2017, "Phoenix Forgotten" received mixed to negative reviews from critics, with many unfavorably comparing it to "The Blair Witch Project." Despite this, the film has found an audience among fans of found footage and science fiction horror, offering a compelling and chilling take on a real-life UFO mystery.

"Phoenix Forgotten" embodies a unique blend of two distinct horror subgenres:

  • Found Footage Horror:

    As a found footage film, "Phoenix Forgotten" utilizes the technique of presenting the story through footage purportedly filmed by the characters themselves. This subgenre creates a sense of immediacy and realism, immersing viewers in the characters' experiences as they explore the mysterious events surrounding the Phoenix Lights phenomenon.

  • Science Fiction Horror:

    The film incorporates elements of science fiction horror, particularly in its exploration of extraterrestrial phenomena and the unknown. The mysterious disappearance of the teenagers and their investigation into the Phoenix Lights incident evoke feelings of fear, suspense, and unease often associated with the science fiction horror genre.
