The Exorcist

The Exorcist is a 1973 American supernatural horror film directed by William Friedkin from a screenplay by William Peter Blatty, based on his 1971 novel. The film stars Ellen Burstyn, Max von Sydow, Jason Miller, and Linda Blair. The story follows the demonic possession of a young girl and her mother's attempt to rescue her through an exorcism by two Catholic priests. Blatty, who also produced, and Friedkin, his choice as director, had difficulty casting the film. Their choice of relative unknowns Burstyn, Blair, and Miller, instead of major stars, drew opposition from Warner Bros. Pictures executives. Principal photography was also difficult, taking place in both hot deserts and refrigerated sets. The Exorcist was released in 24 theaters in the United States on December 26, 1973. The cultural conversation around the film helped it become the first horror film to be nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, as well as nine others. Blatty won Best Adapted Screenplay, while the sound engineers took Best Sound. In 2010, the Library of Congress selected the film for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". .

"The Exorcist" embodies a unique blend of two distinct horror subgenres:

  • Possession Horror:

    "The Exorcist" incorporates the possession subgenre of horror, focusing on the demonic possession of a young girl named Regan MacNeil. As Regan's possession progresses, she exhibits increasingly disturbing behavior and physical transformations, becoming a vessel for malevolent forces. The film explores the terror and psychological turmoil caused by possession, as well as the desperate attempts to exorcise the demon from her body. This aspect of the film contributes to its chilling atmosphere and enduring legacy in the horror genre.

  • Supernatural Horror:

    The film's central premise revolves around the demonic possession of a young girl, Regan MacNeil, and the subsequent exorcism performed by Father Merrin and Father Karras. Supernatural elements such as demonic entities and religious rituals play a significant role in the storyline.

  • Splatter/Exploitation:

    - "Martyrs" qualifies as both a splatter and exploitation film due to its explicit and graphic portrayal of violence and gore. The movie intentionally shocks and repulses the audience, adhering to the characteristics of these subgenres.
