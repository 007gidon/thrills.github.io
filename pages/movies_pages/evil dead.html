The Evil Dead

"The Evil Dead" is a 1981 American supernatural horror film directed by Sam Raimi. It follows five college students who become possessed by demons after discovering an audio tape in an isolated cabin. The film stars Bruce Campbell as Ash Williams, the lone survivor who must battle the demonic forces. Shot on a shoestring budget of $90,000 in a remote cabin in Tennessee, the film's intense filming process proved challenging for the cast and crew. Despite its low budget, "The Evil Dead" gained attention after horror author Stephen King praised it, leading to distribution by New Line Cinema. Grossing $2.4 million in the United States and gaining worldwide recognition, the film became a cult classic and is considered one of the greatest horror films of all time. It launched the careers of Raimi, Campbell, and producer Robert G. Tapert, who went on to create sequels, a soft reboot, a television series, and a fifth film in the franchise, "Evil Dead Rise" in 2023. The film's protagonist, Ash Williams, has become a cultural icon.

"The Evil Dead" embodies a unique blend of two distinct horror subgenres:

  • Possession Horror:

    A central aspect of the film involves demonic possession, as four members of the group become possessed by evil spirits after listening to an audio tape. The possession theme adds a chilling dimension to the horror, as the characters struggle to fend off the malevolent forces controlling them.

  • Supernatural Horror:

    The film primarily falls under the supernatural horror subgenre, as it revolves around demonic possession and the unleashing of evil spirits upon a group of unsuspecting characters.
