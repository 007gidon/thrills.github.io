Children of the Corn

"Children of the Corn" is a 1984 horror film based on the 1977 short story of the same name by Stephen King. Directed by Fritz Kiersch, the film stars Peter Horton and Linda Hamilton. Set in the rural town of Gatlin, Nebraska, the story follows a young couple who become stranded in the town and discover that it is inhabited by a cult of children who worship a malevolent entity known as "He Who Walks Behind the Rows." As the couple, Burt and Vicky, investigate the eerie town, they encounter the sinister and fanatical children who have murdered all the adults in Gatlin as sacrifices to their deity. The film builds tension as Burt and Vicky try to escape the town while being pursued by the cult's leader, Isaac, and his lieutenant, Malachai. "Children of the Corn" explores themes of religious fanaticism, the corrupting influence of power, and the consequences of blind obedience. The film's rural setting and unsettling atmosphere contribute to its sense of dread and unease, while the portrayal of the children as both victims and villains adds depth to the story. Upon its release, "Children of the Corn" received mixed reviews from critics but became a commercial success, spawning numerous sequels and adaptations. Despite its modest budget, the film has endured as a cult classic in the horror genre, known for its memorable characters, iconic quotes, and chilling depiction of small-town terror.

"Children of the Corn" embodies a unique blend of three distinct horror subgenres:

  • Cult Horror:

    The film revolves around a cult of children who worship a malevolent entity and carry out its bidding. The children believe that sacrificing adults to their deity will bring them prosperity and ensure their survival. This theme of cult worship and indoctrination is central to the horror of the film.

  • Supernatural Horror:

    "Children of the Corn" features a supernatural element in the form of "He Who Walks Behind the Rows," an unseen entity that commands the children and enforces their adherence to its will. The entity is shrouded in mystery and fear, adding to the film's sense of unease and dread.
