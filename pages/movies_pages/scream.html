Scream

"Scream" (1996) is a seminal slasher film directed by Wes Craven and written by Kevin Williamson. It follows high school student Sidney Prescott and her friends who become targets of a serial killer known as Ghostface on the anniversary of her mother's murder. The script, originally titled "Scary Movie," satirized slasher film clichés and garnered intense interest from studios. Despite initial doubts, Craven agreed to direct, and filming took place in California with a $15 million budget. Craven had to tone down violence to secure an R-rating from the MPAA. Despite concerns over its release timing, "Scream" defied expectations, grossing around $173 million globally. It received praise for its cast performances and meta-humor but drew criticism for excessive violence. The film revitalized Craven's career and elevated its young cast members. Regarded as one of the greatest horror films, "Scream" revived interest in the genre during the '90s. Its success paved the way for a multimedia franchise, including sequels and a television series, while also making horror more appealing to mainstream actors.

  • Slasher Horror:

    "Scream" (1996) revolutionized the slasher subgenre, blending self-awareness with classic horror tropes to deliver a meta-commentary on the genre itself. Set in the fictional town of Woodsboro, the film follows a masked killer targeting high school students, including protagonist Sidney Prescott. With its iconic Ghostface mask and razor-sharp dialogue, "Scream" pays homage to slasher classics while subverting audience expectations. The film's clever deconstruction of horror conventions, coupled with its blend of suspense and humor, solidified its status as a seminal entry in the slasher genre.
