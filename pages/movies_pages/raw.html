Raw

"Raw" is a 2016 French-Belgian horror film written and directed by Julia Ducournau. The film stars Garance Marillier as Justine, a young vegetarian who discovers a taste for human flesh during her first year at veterinary school. In "Raw," Justine is initially repulsed by meat due to her family's strict vegetarian upbringing. However, during a hazing ritual at the veterinary school, she is forced to eat a raw rabbit kidney, triggering a series of disturbing and cannibalistic cravings. As Justine grapples with her newfound desires, she struggles to maintain control while navigating the challenges of school, peer pressure, and her strained relationship with her older sister, Alexia. The film explores themes of identity, sexuality, and primal urges, using cannibalism as a metaphor for adolescent awakening and rebellion. Ducournau's direction infuses the film with a palpable sense of dread and unease, creating a tense and atmospheric viewing experience. Despite its graphic content and disturbing subject matter, "Raw" resonated with audiences and critics alike, earning praise for its originality and subversive take on the coming-of-age genre. It stands as a standout entry in contemporary horror cinema, offering a fresh and provocative perspective on the human condition.

"Raw" embodies a unique blend of three distinct horror subgenres:

  • Body Horror:

    "Raw" delves deeply into body horror, particularly in its depiction of Justine's cannibalistic urges and the physical transformations she undergoes as a result. The film features graphic and disturbing scenes of flesh consumption, mutilation, and bodily decay, evoking feelings of revulsion and horror.

  • Coming-of-Age Horror:

    At its core, "Raw" is a coming-of-age story, exploring themes of identity, sexuality, and personal transformation. Justine's journey from innocent young student to flesh-craving cannibal serves as a metaphor for the turbulent and often traumatic process of growing up and discovering one's true self.

  • Cannibal Horror:

    As Justine's cravings intensify, "Raw" explores the taboo subject of cannibalism in unsettling detail. The film confronts the audience with the disturbing reality of human flesh consumption, challenging societal taboos and moral boundaries.
